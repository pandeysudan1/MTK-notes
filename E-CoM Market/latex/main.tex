% ── Abstract ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Contemporary electricity markets trade \emph{quantities}---megawatts, megavolt-amperes
reactive, and reserve capacities---while remaining entirely blind to
\emph{where} those quantities sit inside the network.
This proposal introduces the \textbf{Electrical Center-of-Mass (E-CoM)}: a novel
scalar state variable that compresses the spatial distribution of any grid
service into a single, physically interpretable indicator.
Five service centers are defined---Energy, Reactive Power, Inertia, Short-Circuit
Strength, and Flexibility---and a unified dynamics equation governs their
real-time motion.
Building on this theory, four new spatially-aware market mechanisms are proposed:
the Reactive Center Market, the Inertia Center Market, the Voltage Stability
Market (via the Center-of-Mass Stability Index, COMSI), and the Flexibility Center
Market.
An Extended Locational Marginal Price ($\mathrm{LMP}^*$) augments the classical
three-part decomposition with a spatial-value component $\lambda_{\mathrm{CoM}}$.
A real-time digital twin architecture tracks center movements, provides
multi-horizon forecasts, and translates spatial imbalances into pre-dispatch
signals for system operators.
The 2--3 year research programme is structured across five work packages,
culminating in a demonstration on the Nordic power system in collaboration with
Statnett, SvK, and Fingrid.
\end{abstract}

\section{The State of Today's Electricity Markets}
\label{sec:problem_markets}

Modern energy markets have evolved to trade an increasingly rich basket of
products \citep{entsoe2022marketreport}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Active Power (MW)} and \textbf{Energy (MWh)} via day-ahead and
        intraday auctions.
  \item \textbf{Reactive Power (MVAr)} through bilateral contracts or optional
        ancillary-service tenders.
  \item \textbf{Reserve Capacity} (FCR, aFRR, mFRR) for frequency regulation.
  \item \textbf{Frequency services} including synthetic inertia and fast
        frequency response.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Despite this breadth, one critical dimension is universally absent from market
pricing:

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=red!5!white, colframe=red!60!black, fonttitle=\bfseries,
                  title=The Fundamental Gap]
\textit{Markets price quantity. They are blind to where that quantity sits in
the network.}
No current market product explicitly rewards a resource for its \textbf{spatial
relationship} to system needs.
\end{tcolorbox}

What markets currently \emph{ignore}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Spatial balance of resources relative to load.
  \item Electrical distance between supply and demand.
  \item Dynamic system strength distribution.
  \item Inertia distribution across the network.
  \item Voltage-support location relative to stressed buses.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Why Location Matters: The Hidden Instability}
\label{sec:why_location}

The absence of spatial market signals creates real-world stability
vulnerabilities that are growing as inverter-based renewables displace
synchronous generators \citep{milano2018inertia}.

\subsection{Illustrative Counter-example}

Consider two power grids with \emph{identical} aggregate MW and MVAr totals.
Grid A has its synchronous generation and reactive compensation distributed
uniformly across the network.
Grid B has its generation concentrated in one corner.
Classical markets cannot distinguish between these two systems and will clear
them at the same prices, yet their stability characteristics differ drastically:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Inertia concentrated in one corner} $\Rightarrow$ frequency
        collapse in remote areas following a generation trip.
  \item \textbf{Reactive power far from stressed buses} $\Rightarrow$ voltage
        instability despite sufficient aggregate MVAr.
  \item \textbf{Flexibility assets in the wrong location} $\Rightarrow$
        congestion persists despite surplus aggregate capacity.
\end{itemize}

The spatial mismatch is invisible to current market mechanisms, so no economic
signal drives resources toward the locations where stability is threatened.

\section{The Central Research Hypothesis}
\label{sec:hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}
Every power-system service possesses a \textbf{spatial center} whose movement
governs stability, efficiency, and economic value.
Designing markets around the alignment and dynamics of these centers will
create economic incentives that naturally drive the grid toward secure, stable,
and efficient operating states.
\end{hypothesis}

This hypothesis generates three concrete research contributions:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em, style=nextline]
  \item[New State Variable]
        One number per service captures the spatial balance of the entire
        network, reducing thousands of bus measurements to a physically
        interpretable scalar.
  \item[New Market Signal]
        Payments reward \emph{where} a resource is located relative to system
        needs, not only \emph{how much} it provides.
  \item[New Control Layer]
        A digital twin tracks center movements in real time and issues
        location-aware pre-dispatch instructions to system operators.
\end{description}

\section{Electrical Center-of-Mass: Formal Definition}
\label{sec:ecom_def}

\begin{definition}[Electrical Center-of-Mass]
\label{def:ecom}
For a power system with node set $\mathcal{N}$ and a grid service $s$, the
\textbf{Electrical Center-of-Mass} at time $t$ is the service-weighted spatial
centroid:
\begin{equation}
  X_s(t) \;=\; \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} w_i^{(s)}(t)\, x_i}
                     {\displaystyle\sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} w_i^{(s)}(t)}
  \label{eq:ecom}
\end{equation}
where $x_i$ is the electrical location of node $i$ (e.g.\ a spectral graph
embedding \citep{fiedler1973algebraic}), and $w_i^{(s)}(t)$ is the contribution
of node $i$ to service $s$ at time $t$.
\end{definition}

\noindent
The variable $X_s(t)$ is a scalar (or low-dimensional vector in 2-D embeddings)
that summarises the entire spatial distribution of service $s$ across the grid
at a single instant.

\subsection*{Algorithm: Computing the E-CoM}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Computing the Electrical Center-of-Mass},
                   label={lst:ecom}]
def CoM(service, nodes, time):
    num   = 0
    denom = 0
    for node i in nodes:
        w      = weight(service, i, time)   # service contribution at i
        num   += w * location(i)             # electrical location
        denom += w
    return num / denom                       # X_s(t)
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Center-of-Mass Dynamics: A New State Equation}
\label{sec:ecom_dynamics}

Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:ecom} with respect to time yields the
\textbf{CoM dynamics equation}:

\begin{equation}
  \dot{X}_s(t) \;=\;
  \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i} \dot{w}_i^{(s)}\, x_i}
       {\displaystyle\sum_{i} w_i^{(s)}}
  \;-\;
  X_s(t)\,\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i} \dot{w}_i^{(s)}}
               {\displaystyle\sum_{i} w_i^{(s)}}
  \label{eq:ecom_dynamics}
\end{equation}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item[$\dot{X}_s > 0$] Center drifting in the positive spatial direction
        (e.g.\ eastward on a geographic embedding).
  \item[$\dot{X}_s < 0$] Center drifting in the negative spatial direction.
  \item[$\dot{X}_s = 0$] System is spatially balanced for service $s$.
\end{description}

Equation~\eqref{eq:ecom_dynamics} forms the state-space backbone of the
digital twin layer (Section~\ref{sec:digital_twin}), enabling prediction of
$X_s(t+h)$ hours ahead from measured weight trajectories.

\section{From Quantity-Only to Spatially-Aware Markets}
\label{sec:market_principle}

The core market-design innovation is to augment the standard
\emph{price $\times$ quantity} payment with a \emph{spatial value factor}:

\begin{align}
  \text{Current:}\quad   & \text{Payment} = \text{Price} \times Q \\[4pt]
  \text{Proposed:}\quad  & \text{Payment} = \text{Price} \times Q
                            \times f\!\left(d\right)
  \label{eq:spatial_payment}
\end{align}

where $d = \lvert X_{\text{service}} - X_{\text{need}} \rvert$ is the
distance between the center of supply and the center of need.
Resources \emph{closer} to where the system needs support receive a
location premium; resources further away receive a discount.

\section{Market 1: Reactive Center Market}
\label{sec:reactive_market}

Today, operators pay for reactive quantity only.
A reactive source far from voltage-stressed buses earns the same payment as one
co-located with the stress, despite providing far less voltage support.

The Reactive Center Market rewards providers according to the shift they create
in the reactive CoM toward the centre of reactive demand:

\begin{equation}
  \text{bid}(i) = \lambda_Q \cdot Q_i \cdot \text{bonus}\!\left(
    \lVert X_Q^{+\Delta Q_i} - X_{\text{stress}} \rVert
    - \lVert X_Q - X_{\text{stress}} \rVert
  \right)
\end{equation}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Reactive Center Market dispatch},
                   label={lst:reactive}]
def ReactiveMarket(nodes, X_stress):
    X_Q = CoM(Q, nodes)
    for node i in nodes:
        X_new  = CoM(Q + dQ_i, nodes)         # CoM after i's contribution
        shift  = dist(X_new, X_stress) \
               - dist(X_Q,   X_stress)         # negative = improvement
        bid[i] = price_Q * Q_i * bonus(shift)  # premium for large shift
    return dispatch(bid)
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Payment reward:} MVAr + $\Delta X_Q$ (Reactive CoM Shift toward stress).

\section{Market 2: Inertia Center Market}
\label{sec:inertia_market}

The inertia center is:
\begin{equation}
  X_H = \frac{\sum_i H_i\, x_i}{\sum_i H_i}
\end{equation}

With the rapid displacement of synchronous generators by inverter-based
resources, system operators must procure inertia both by \emph{quantity} and
by \emph{location} \citep{milano2018inertia}.
Hydropower plants providing synchronous inertia near electrically weak zones
are worth significantly more than equivalent inertia delivered from a
well-connected hub, yet today they earn the same capacity payment.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Inertia Center Market dispatch},
                   label={lst:inertia}]
def InertiaMarket(nodes, X_need):
    X_H = CoM(H, nodes)
    for node i in nodes:
        d       = dist(location(i), X_need)
        sv      = spatialBonus(d)
        payment[i] = price_H * H_i * sv
    return dispatch(payment)
\end{lstlisting}

Under this mechanism, hydropower transitions from a pure energy source to a
\textbf{spatial stability asset}, earning a premium commensurate with its
inertia contribution at a critical network location.

\section{Market 3: Voltage Stability Market via COMSI}
\label{sec:voltage_market}

A new system-wide voltage stability index is defined as:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{COMSI} = 1 - \frac{\lvert X_Q - X_L \rvert}{L}
  \label{eq:comsi}
\end{equation}

where $X_Q$ is the center of reactive supply, $X_L$ is the center of reactive
demand, and $L$ is the system characteristic length (normaliser).
$\mathrm{COMSI} \to 1$ indicates a perfectly matched, spatially balanced system;
$\mathrm{COMSI} \to 0$ indicates a critically mismatched system approaching
voltage instability.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Voltage Stability Market via COMSI},
                   label={lst:comsi}]
def VoltageStabilityMarket(nodes):
    COMSI_base = computeCOMSI(nodes)
    for node i in nodes:
        COMSI_new = computeCOMSI(nodes + contribution(i))
        delta     = COMSI_new - COMSI_base   # positive = improved stability
        reward[i] = stabPrice * delta
    return dispatch(reward)
\end{lstlisting}

Resources that improve COMSI earn a stability premium proportional to the
improvement they deliver to the system as a whole.

\section{Market 4: Flexibility Center Market}
\label{sec:flex_market}

The flexibility center:
\begin{equation}
  X_F = \frac{\sum_i F_i\, x_i}{\sum_i F_i}, \quad
  F_i \in \{\text{EV}, \text{BESS}, \text{DR}\}
\end{equation}

Today, TSOs purchase flexibility by volume alone.
A battery in a congested zone is purchased at the same price as one in an
unconstrained zone, so congestion persists despite surplus aggregate capacity.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Flexibility Center Market dispatch},
                   label={lst:flex}]
def FlexMarket(nodes, target):
    X_F = CoM(F, nodes)
    for node i in nodes:
        shift     = CoMShift(F_i, location(i), X_F, target)
        payment[i] = price_F * F_i * locationBonus(shift)
    return dispatch(payment)
\end{lstlisting}

Flexibility assets near congested or voltage-stressed zones earn a location
premium, incentivising storage deployment and demand-response enrolment in
exactly the locations the network most needs them.

\section{Extended Locational Marginal Pricing ($\mathrm{LMP}^*$)}
\label{sec:lmp_star}

Classical LMP decomposes nodal prices into three components
\citep{schweppe1988spot}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{LMP} = \lambda_E + \lambda_L + \lambda_C
\end{equation}

The proposed \emph{Extended LMP} augments this with a spatial-value component:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \mathrm{LMP}^* = \lambda_E + \lambda_L + \lambda_C + \lambda_{\mathrm{CoM}}
  }
  \label{eq:lmp_star}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{\mathrm{CoM}} = \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} \pi_s \cdot \Delta X_s(i)
\end{equation}
$\Delta X_s(i)$ is the CoM shift that node $i$ induces for service $s$, and
$\pi_s$ is the shadow price of spatial balance for that service.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Extended LMP* computation},
                   label={lst:lmpstar}]
def extendedLMP(node_i, grid):
    lmp_E   = energyComp(node_i)
    lmp_L   = lossComp(node_i)
    lmp_C   = congestionComp(node_i)
    lmp_CoM = 0
    for service s in services:
        dX       = CoMShift(s, node_i, grid)
        lmp_CoM += price(s) * dX         # spatial value contribution
    return lmp_E + lmp_L + lmp_C + lmp_CoM
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent
$\lambda_{\mathrm{CoM}}$ makes a resource's full spatial contribution visible in
its nodal price for the first time.

\section{The Digital Twin: A Living Map of Grid Balance}
\label{sec:digital_twin}

\subsection{Motivation}

A modern TSO control room displays 20,000+ buses and 50,000+ simultaneous
measurements. No single indicator currently conveys spatial balance; instability
is often detected only after it begins. The CoM digital twin replaces this data
deluge with five meaningful time-varying scalars.

\subsection{Architecture}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Grid Digital Twin: real-time estimator and forecaster},
                   label={lst:twin}]
class GridDigitalTwin:

    def init(self, grid_topology):
        self.topology = grid_topology
        self.centers  = {}    # { service : X_s(t) }
        self.history  = []    # list of (t, centers) for forecasting

    def update(self, measurements, t):
        for s in [Energy, Q, H, F, Storage]:
            weights          = extractWeights(measurements, s)
            self.centers[s]  = CoM(weights, self.topology)
        self.history.append((t, self.centers.copy()))

    def forecast(self, horizon_hours):
        model = fitDynamics(self.history)   # Kalman filter or ML model
        return model.predict(horizon_hours)

    def dispatchSignal(self, target_centers):
        # returns location-aware pre-dispatch recommendations
        gaps = { s: target_centers[s] - self.centers[s]
                 for s in self.centers }
        return optimiseDispatch(gaps, self.topology)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Dashboard Outputs}

The digital twin continuously publishes five center tracks to the operator
dashboard:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Energy Center} $X_E$ --- moves with real-time dispatch.
  \item \textbf{Reactive Center} $X_Q$ --- tracks voltage support location.
  \item \textbf{Inertia Center} $X_H$ --- reveals inertia geography.
  \item \textbf{Flexibility Center} $X_F$ --- tracks DR/BESS/EV deployment.
  \item \textbf{Congestion Center} --- locates current bottlenecks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{WP5: Hydropower as a Spatial Stabiliser}
\label{sec:hydropower}

\subsection{Research Question}
\textit{How does hydropower shift the energy, inertia, and reactive centers
during disturbances and market operation, and what location-aware market premium
does this imply?}

\subsection{Strategic Importance of Hydropower}
Hydropower is uniquely positioned as a spatial stabiliser:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Provides \emph{both} energy and synchronous inertia simultaneously.
  \item Geographically distributed in mountainous, often electrically remote
        regions (Norway, Sweden, Nepal, Albania).
  \item Fast ramp rates $\Rightarrow$ strong CoM control authority.
  \item Currently \emph{severely under-valued} in today's markets because spatial
        contribution is not monetised.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Hydropower CoM shift analysis},
                   label={lst:hydro}]
def HydroCoMAnalysis(disturbance):
    for t in timeline:
        X_H  = CoM(H, nodes, t)            # inertia center
        X_E  = CoM(P, nodes, t)            # energy center
        X_Q  = CoM(Q, nodes, t)            # reactive center
        log(t, X_H, X_E, X_Q)
    COMSI_integral = integrate(COMSI(t), timeline)
    return marketPremium(hydro, COMSI_integral)
\end{lstlisting}

The integral of COMSI over an event horizon provides a single number capturing
the total voltage-stability contribution of each hydro plant, forming the basis
for a location-aware capacity payment.
